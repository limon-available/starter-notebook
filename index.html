Abstract

This study asks which content pages should be reviewed first for potential search-visibility decline using observable historical search-performance signals. A Random Forest classifier was evaluated with a time-aware design using an April 30, 2026 training snapshot and a June 30, 2026 test snapshot, with three temporally aligned features: imp_prev30, clk_last30, and pos_last30. The decline label is a proxy defined as imp_last30 < 0.8 × imp_prev30, and the audited model measured 0.709 ROC-AUC and 60.85% accuracy, compared with a 64.31% majority-class accuracy reference. The resulting output is therefore framed as a directional, research-stage review-prioritization signal rather than a reliable standalone predictor or automatic content-action system.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Search-performance data can contain signals that a content team may want to inspect, but reviewing every content page in the same order is inefficient. This project asks:

Which content pages should be reviewed first for potential search-visibility decline?

The decision supported by this work is human review prioritization. The workflow ranks records by model score and provides simple reason codes that help a reviewer understand which observable signals deserve inspection.

The goal is not to predict Google's ranking algorithm, prove a causal explanation for a decline, or automatically change content. It is to create a repeatable decision-support workflow using observable historical performance signals.

2. Data

This study uses the FlyRank internship warehouse release available through Hugging Face. The main modeling analysis uses daily search-performance data.

Tables used

  • fact_content_daily_performance — daily search-performance signals.
  • fact_content_query_90d — query-level aggregate signals used in earlier exploratory analysis.
  • dim_clients — client-level identifiers used for grouping and validation.
  • dim_content — content-level identifiers and metadata.

Time windows

Apr 30, 2026 Training snapshot
Jun 30, 2026 Test snapshot
30 + 30 days Historical comparison windows

For each snapshot, features are constructed from historical performance windows available at that snapshot. Records with imp_prev30 < 100 are excluded because the analysis requires a minimum previous-period impression volume.

The label is_declining is constructed using the most recent 30-day impression window relative to the previous 30-day window. The imp_last30 value is therefore excluded from the model features because it directly defines the label.

Query-derived features used in earlier analysis were excluded from the final audited model because their available 90-day window could not be historically aligned to the April 30, 2026 training cutoff.

Client names, domains, private queries, credentials, and raw client-level exports are not included in this public-facing paper.

3. Methodology

Assumptions

Observable historical search-performance signals are treated as decision-support signals for prioritizing content pages for human review. The decline label is treated as a proxy rather than a direct measurement of content quality or future traffic loss.

Features

  • imp_prev30 — impressions from the previous 30-day window.
  • clk_last30 — clicks from the most recent 30-day observation window.
  • pos_last30 — average search position from the most recent 30-day observation window.

Label definition

is_declining = (imp_last30 < 0.8 × imp_prev30)

A page is labeled as declining when its most recent 30-day impressions are less than 80% of its previous 30-day impressions. This is an observed proxy label, not a direct measure of content quality.

Baseline

The Week-4 baseline used a rule-based ranking approach combining impression drop and top-query share. Pages with larger impression drops and higher query concentration received higher review priority. The available baseline was a ranking rule and did not produce directly comparable classification metrics in the final audited analysis.

Model

The final audited model is a Random Forest classifier using the three temporally safe features listed above. Random Forest was selected because it can capture non-linear relationships between search-performance features and the decline proxy label.

Validation design

An earlier Week-5 evaluation used a client-grouped split based on client_hash_id. A stricter time-aware validation was then used for the research claim audit:

  • Training snapshot: April 30, 2026
  • Test snapshot: June 30, 2026

Leakage checks

  1. imp_last30 was excluded because it is part of the label definition.
  2. Future-window features were excluded from the training features.
  3. Query-derived features were excluded when their available window could not be historically aligned to training.
  4. Private client information and raw exports were excluded from the public-facing analysis.

The final feature set is therefore limited to historically aligned performance signals available at the relevant prediction snapshot.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

What the work cannot claim

  • It cannot claim to predict Google's ranking algorithm.
  • It cannot establish that content quality caused an observed decline.
  • It cannot establish that refreshing content will improve traffic or rankings.
  • It cannot claim reliable standalone prediction of future decline.
  • It cannot claim that the ranked recommendations are optimal content decisions.
  • It cannot treat the observed relationships as causal.

The decline label is only an impression-change proxy. Search performance can also be affected by seasonality, changes in search intent, temporary ranking fluctuations, user interest, and other factors that are not represented by the final feature set.

The final audited model uses only imp_prev30, clk_last30, and pos_last30. The public-safe analysis also excludes client names, content names, private queries, URLs, credentials, and raw exports.

The findings should therefore be described as observed, measured, directional, and decision-support evidence, rather than causal or production-level conclusions.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The action playbook ranks test-snapshot records by the Random Forest's predicted probability of belonging to the decline proxy class. A higher decline_probability places a record earlier in the review queue.

Priority Recommendation Reason
1 Review highest-ranked records first Higher predicted decline probability
2 Review visibility signals Check prior-period impression levels
3 Review engagement signals Check recent click performance
4 Review position signals Inspect relatively weak average position
5 Review multiple-signal records Multiple observable review signals are present
6 General review Inspect high-score records without a specific reason code

Reason codes

  • REVIEW_VISIBILITY — prior-period impression signal is relatively low.
  • REVIEW_ENGAGEMENT — recent click signal is relatively low.
  • REVIEW_POSITION — position signal is relatively weak when available.
  • MULTIPLE_REVIEW_SIGNALS — more than one review signal is present.
  • GENERAL_REVIEW — no specific review signal was assigned.

Human review process

  1. Inspect the historical search-performance signals.
  2. Check whether the observed change is meaningful or temporary.
  3. Consider context not represented by the model.
  4. Determine whether a content review is appropriate.
  5. Take action only after human review.
No automatic actions: every ranked record is assigned HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED. The queue is a review-prioritization playbook, not an automatic content-action system.
